# Question bank

Pick what fits their field. Adapt wording to trades, healthcare, retail, education, office, technical, or anything else. Ask one at a time.

## Phase 1: the job they want
- In your own words, what kind of job are you looking for?
- Same field as your last job, or a change? If a change, what pulls you toward it?
- What titles have you seen that sound right? (offer 2 to 4 from the resume)
- What level: entry, experienced, lead or supervisor, manager or above?
- Full time, part time, contract, seasonal? Any schedule you need (days, nights, weekends, set hours)?
- Remote, hybrid, on site? What is the longest commute you would accept?
- How much travel is fine, if any?
- Company size or type you prefer or avoid (small family business, large company, nonprofit, government, union)?
- What did you like most and least about your last two jobs?

## Phase 3: constraints and dealbreakers
- Where are you based, and would you relocate? Under what conditions?
- Are you working now? How soon could you start, and do you need to give notice?
- Licenses, certifications, clearances, or a driver's license you hold? Any expiring soon?
- Anything you will not do (night shifts, heavy lifting, sales quotas, on call, background check items)?
- Anything a posting could say that makes you want to skip it immediately?

## Phase 4: experience (per recent role)
- What was the job in one sentence, and who did you report to?
- What did a normal week look like?
- How big was the scope: people managed or served, accounts, equipment, budget, volume, sites?
- What are you proudest of there? What changed because of you?
- Something you fixed or improved. What was broken, what did you do, what happened after?
- A number you can give: sales, units, customers per day, errors cut, time saved, safety record.
- Any awards, promotions, or praise from a boss or customer?
- What tools, machines, software, or systems did you use daily?
- Why did you leave, or why are you leaving? (Frame calmly; this feeds the cover letter.)
- Older roles: one or two questions each. What did you do, and what skill carried forward?

## Phase 5: strengths and competency matrix
- Top five to eight skills a new employer would pay for. For each: Expert, Working, or Exposure, one example, which roles used it, and when you last used it.
- A time you trained or helped someone else.
- Anything you do well that is not on the resume?

## Phase 6: stories, data points, wins, and losses
Stories (3 to 5, ask for each: situation, task, what YOU did, result, what you learned; tag them leadership, conflict, failure, customer, pressure, change, teaching):
- Tell me about a time you solved a problem nobody else could.
- A hard situation (angry customer, deadline, conflict, emergency) and how it ended.
- A time you changed how something was done.
- A time you led, trained, or influenced people without being the boss.

Data points (collect every number they can give, with its source or `~estimate`):
- Volume (customers per day, units, accounts, tickets, patients, orders), team or budget size, years on each tool or task.
- Before and after figures: time saved, errors cut, revenue or cost, ratings, audit or safety results, attendance or retention.
- Rankings, quotas hit, rates (on time, first try, satisfaction).

Wins:
- Your three biggest wins. For each: what changed because of you, and who noticed (boss, customer, award, raise, promotion)?
- Praise, awards, reviews, thank-you notes you can point to. Where are they saved?

Losses (calibrated and honest; these become interview answers):
- A project, goal, or decision that failed or fell short. What went wrong, what was your part, what did you change after?
- A mistake that cost time or money, and how you fixed it.
- A job, layoff, firing, or gap you will be asked about. What is the plain, calm version?
- A skill you wish you were stronger in, and what you are doing about it.

## Probing patterns
- "How many?" "How often?" "Compared to what?" "What happened after?"
- "You said we. What was your part?"
- "Is that about 10, 50, or 200?" (range menus)
- "Where could we find that number later (old review, pay stub, boss)?"
